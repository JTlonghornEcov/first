United Kingdom
 

DRS registration is now open

Exchange for Change, the body running the UK Deposit Return Scheme, has opened Phase 1 registration for drinks producers and retailer return points. Phase 1 only asks for basic details: company information, a primary contact, trading name and addresses. You'll get a registration reference number once you submit.

Phase 2 opens in January 2027 and will ask for more detail. The scheme goes live in October 2027. Small retailers who may qualify for a return point exemption still need to register.

  What to do: If you place drinks containers on the UK market, register now. Your volume and product data will be needed for Phase 2, so start pulling it together.

DRS producer fees set at 0p for the first 15 months

Exchange for Change has confirmed stepped producer fees. Fees for all drinks containers will be 0p for the first 15 months of the scheme, and will be reviewed and reconfirmed in May 2027. That's welcome news for budgets, but registration, labelling and data obligations still apply from day one.

1 October: EPR deadline for large producers

1 October was the deadline for large producers to submit January–June 2026 packaging data and complete their 2027 registration. If you've missed it, contact your environmental regulator as soon as possible.

For the next submission (July–December data, due 1 April 2027), keep building up your nation of sale and self-managed waste data. The grace period on enforcing these has been extended, so you won't be penalised for gaps yet, but the data still needs to be reported and kept on record. Using the extra time to get it right now will make future submissions much easier.

Kosovo moves a step closer to EPR for packaging and tyres

[Draft for policy team review TBC]

Kosovo has completed a year-long project, backed by the German government through GIZ, to design EPR models for packaging waste and tyres. The work brought together the Ministry of Environment, Spatial Planning and Infrastructure, business associations and civil society. It mapped stakeholders, estimated how much packaging and how many tyres are placed on the market each year, and produced a roadmap for putting the schemes into practice.

It builds on earlier EPR design work for electricals, batteries, used oils and end-of-life vehicles, and supports Kosovo's Integrated Waste Management Strategy 2024–2035. Obligations aren't in force yet, as a dedicated EPR law is still going through approval. But the direction is clear. If you sell into Kosovo or the wider Western Balkans, now is the time to understand what you place on the market there.

United States
 

Oregon's EPR law upheld in court

On 27 August, a federal court upheld Oregon's packaging EPR law in full, after a challenge from the National Association of Wholesaler-Distributors. It's the first ruling on the merits of a state packaging EPR law. The injunction that had shielded the challengers' members has been lifted, so the law now applies to all covered producers.

The ruling matters beyond Oregon. Similar challenges are under way in California and Colorado, and most state schemes use the same model. An appeal is still possible, but the message is clear: don't wait for the courts. If you sell packaged goods into US EPR states, your reporting and fee obligations stand.

New Jersey delays recycled content rules for food packaging to 2029

New Jersey has extended the exemption for food-contact packaging under its Recycled Content Law. Requirements that were due on 18 January 2027 now start on 31 December 2029, citing limited supply of food-safe recycled material. Beverage containers aren't covered by the extension, and other rigid plastic containers still face higher recycled content targets from 2027.

  What to do: Use the extra time to check your supply chain's access to food-grade recycled content, rather than pausing the work.
