California · SB 54

California: reduction targets with an exponentially increasing bill

California's SB 54 is the most ambitious US EPR law, and the only one to write plastic reduction targets directly into statute: a minimum 25% plastic source reduction from 2023 levels by 2032. These targets are the cornerstone of the state's eco-modulation framework, which consists of four main elements.

  1. 01

    2023 baseline report

    A report on packaging placed on the California market in 2023, including details on plastic components and weights, to function as the starting point for all future reductions.

  2. 02

    Source Reduction Plan

    A roadmap of methods and timelines that is not binding but acts as an indication of the steps which will be taken to reduce the amount of plastic used in packaging.

  3. 03

    Annual source reduction reports

    Submitted on a regular basis to the PRO (CAA) alongside the usual annual supply reports. They measure reduction progress against the 2023 baseline, and are used alongside the supply reports to calculate the amount of reduction implemented.

  4. 04

    Source Reduction Incentive Mechanism (SRIM)

    CAA's mechanism to translate implemented plastic reduction into lower fees. As the reduction targets from SB 54 are binding on the PRO, rather than individual producers, the SRIM incentivises individual responsibility, with the aim of collectively meeting these targets.

How the SRIM bill grows

The SRIM is a carrot-and-stick system, but the system works reactively to increase maluses for producers lagging behind the majority. Bonuses are funded directly by maluses, so the size of the bonus pool determines the size of everyone else's bill.

$10 million in bonuses split across 10,000 producers leaves a malus of $1,000 per producer. When only 1,000 producers are left paying, the same pool means $10,000 each; with 100 left, it is $100,000 each.

The more producers that earn a bonus, the more expensive it becomes to keep packaging the same.

Colorado

Colorado: material-level factors and producer-level bonuses

Colorado's approach is more traditional, applying a combination of passive and active eco-modulation factors to incentivise the use of more sustainable packaging. Notably, Colorado's eco-modulation applies to all materials.

Passive factors

Passive factors apply uniform factors across a whole material reporting category after base fees are calculated, drawing on the scoring from the state's Needs Assessment. In the first programme year, the malus raises base fees (excluding reserves) by 5% for the applicable category, and the incentive lowers them by 5%.

There is also a tiered malus for materials outside the Minimum Recycling List (MRL). Materials on the Additional Materials List (AML) must be priced at least 20% above the average for similar MRL materials, and non-collected materials at least 10% above similar AML materials.

Active factors

Active factors reward individual producer practices that cannot be assessed at material level: source reduction, recyclability innovation, high PCR content, and reuse and refill.

  • Source reduction
  • Recyclability innovation
  • High PCR content
  • Reuse and refill

Producers submit additional data through the portal, which is then verified, and thresholds are informed by an independent technical committee. The data burden is considerable, but so is the opportunity to earn a fee reduction. Colorado also plans to refine the system annually, so these benchmarks will move.

Oregon

Oregon: bonuses for life cycle assessment

Oregon has built its system around third-party reviewed Life Cycle Assessments (LCAs), all conducted under OAR 340-090-0900 to 0940 and the EU's Product Environmental Footprint method, covering 16 impact categories.

  • A

    Bonus A: LCA disclosure

    Rewards voluntary LCA disclosure with 10% of base fees (excluding reserves) for the primary packaging in a SKU or batch of related SKUs, capped at $20,000 per SKU or batch. With a maximum of 10 SKUs or batches, that adds up to $200,000 in total.

    Up to$200,000

  • B

    Bonus B: lower impact

    Rewards comparative LCAs showing reduced environmental impact of a primary packaging system, tiered by reduction: up to $40,000 for 10 to 40%, $45,000 for 40 to 70%, and $50,000 above 70%. The total across ten SKUs or batches is up to $500,000, credited against future fees.
